Kohl's introduced buy online, ship to store to improve shopping both online and in store. It was an omni-channel project spanning four channels: desktop, mobile browser, native app and tablet.

At its core, this was a question of **where and when**: helping someone choose a store and a date that work for them, then giving them everything they need to show up with confidence.

<Callout title="Design themes">
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Choosing where and when</strong> — location and date options shown at the moment of decision, in plain language.</li>
    <li><strong>A confirmation that gets people there</strong> — where to go, when it's open, and how long it will wait for them.</li>
    <li><strong>One experience across online and in person</strong> — consistent across 4 digital channels and the store itself.</li>
  </ul>
</Callout>

<Section eyebrow="Design walkthrough" title="First stop: browsing">
  <p>The product matrix page is the first stop when customers consider buying from Kohl's. The goal was simple: <strong>make the service visible from the very beginning, so it can influence purchase decisions.</strong></p>
  <Figure src="/images/boss-pmp.svg" alt="Product matrix page with ship-to-store availability shown on product tiles." />
  <p>Mobile has the least room, so we first focused on fitting the experience into mobile. We explored rounds of designs and brought customers into the testing lab for feedback.</p>
  <Figure src="/images/boss-currentdesign.svg" alt="The product matrix page before the change." caption="Before" />
  <Figure src="/images/boss-pmpiterations.svg" alt="Mobile concept iterations tested with customers, with rationale for each." caption="Iterations" />
  <Figure src="/images/boss-pmpfinal.svg" alt="Final mobile product matrix design." caption="Final design" />

<Section eyebrow="Design walkthrough" title="Second stop: choosing where and when">
  <p>The product details page is where customers decide how and where to get their item. Many Kohl's customers are older or less tech-savvy — something that's easy to overlook in Silicon Valley — so every option had to be clear without extra effort. Designing this page was a good exercise in empathy.</p>
  <Figure src="/images/boss-pdp.svg" alt="Product details page showing ship-to-store as a delivery option." />
  <p>Because of operational constraints, shipping options can't be shown until a size and color are selected. Instead of disabling "Add to Cart", we kept it always enabled and opened the size and color panel when needed. In testing, a disabled button made some customers think they'd done something wrong.</p>
