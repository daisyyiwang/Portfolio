Kohl's Yes2You (Y2Y) rewards program let shoppers without a Kohl's Charge card earn points off. Introduced about three years earlier, it won over two kinds of customers: people who love to shop and save at Kohl's but don't want another credit card, and potential cardholders just starting to shop with Kohl's.

But while it kept many new shoppers, its complicated points system kept many more away. To simplify it, Kohl's loyalty team came to us with an exciting proposal: **build a unified rewards platform based on Kohl's Cash.**

<Section eyebrow="My role" title="From pitch to core mobile flow">
  <p>I helped shape the initial design pitch that got the new program funded by stakeholders, then built the first draft of the core mobile flow with 2 other experience designers, a visual designer, a design manager and a project planner.</p>
</Section>

<Section eyebrow="Design workshop" title="Gather all the thoughts up front">
  <p>Collaborating with so many partners on a big project is hard, so our research team ran a design workshop to align expectations early. With Business Credit, Product, Store Operations, Creative and Marketing, we analyzed the current program and agreed on what the future one should be:</p>
  <Callout title="Key takeaways">
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Transparent:</strong> effortless to understand and use.</li>
      <li><strong>Informative:</strong> consolidate earning methods into a single window on earnings and savings.</li>
      <li><strong>Celebratory:</strong> use our data for personalized offers, and celebrate past, present and future earnings.</li>
    </ul>
  </Callout>
  <Quote emoji="💬">"Kohl's Cash is more talked about than Y2Y. It has a larger impact on customers' long-term behavior — 2× more likely to drive upward migration."</Quote>
</Section>

<Section eyebrow="Design iterations" title="Order confirmation flow">
  <Figure src="/images/loyalty-oc03.svg" alt="Order confirmation iterations, from a celebratory modal to an in-page animation." />
  <p>A key workshop insight was to bring celebration into the experience, so one order-confirmation design opened with a celebratory modal. It was well received internally — but in the lab, customers were more confused than delighted. That finding aligned the team, and I replaced the modal with a brief in-page animation. It's one of many examples of refining concepts through customer feedback.</p>
</Section>

<Section eyebrow="Design reflection" title="Design your process">
  <h3>Hi-fi or lo-fi?</h3>
  <p>At first I wasn't sure what fidelity to bring to which meeting. My takeaways: hi-fi works best for pitching concepts to business stakeholders (with the branding team, if there is one), and for usability testing. For product and engineering, lo-fi wireframes with watermarks avoid confusion with final deliverables.</p>
  <h3>Document your process</h3>
  <p>With many designers on a long project, it's easy to get lost across tools, meetings and notes. Documenting the process in one consistent place, and working closely with your planner, helps a lot.</p>
  <h3>Explore as a discussion point</h3>
  <p>With 2 design studios at Kohl's, remote collaboration was a given — and it's hard to pitch ideas without visualizing them. I also find it hard to reach good concepts without exploring "bad" ones. Working through those ideas with partners makes the final proposal more convincing.</p>
</Section>
