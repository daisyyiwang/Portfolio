Kohl's

Buy online, ship to store

Bridging the gap between online and in-store shopping across four channels.

  • B2C
  • eCommerce
  • Logistics
Role
Experience designer
Launched
Pilot, Aug 2018
Audience
Shoppers across 4 channels

Kohl’s introduced buy online, ship to store to improve shopping both online and in store. It was an omni-channel project spanning four channels: desktop, mobile browser, native app and tablet.

My role

Four verbs

I explored content strategies and wireframe concepts to translate this new service for every kind of customer, across channels.

I evaluated the touchpoints on the customer journey — when customers want to be informed, and in how much detail — with 5 channel designers and 2 product managers.

I examined whether the experience stayed consistent in store and online, balancing business goals with customer behavior.

I empowered product and engineering to ship the mobile experience smoothly for the pilot launch and beyond.

Design touchpoints

Mapping out the customer journey

With the problem space and business value defined, we stepped back to look at the customer journey and find the key touchpoints during online shopping. The simplified map below shows why we started with three pages. Since this was an omni-channel solution, we designed for both desktop and mobile; I’ve picked three examples from across channels to show how I made design decisions.

Design walkthrough

First stop: browsing

The product matrix page is the first stop when customers consider buying from Kohl’s. The goal was simple: make the service visible from the very beginning, so it can influence purchase decisions.

Mobile has the least room, so we first focused on fitting the experience into mobile. We explored rounds of designs and brought customers into the testing lab for feedback.

Design walkthrough

Second stop: placing an order

The product details page helps customers decide to buy. Many Kohl’s customers are older or less tech-savvy — something that’s easy to overlook in Silicon Valley. Designing this page was a good exercise in empathy.

Because of operational constraints, shipping options can’t be shown until a size and color are selected. Instead of disabling “Add to Cart”, we kept it always enabled and opened the size and color panel when needed. In testing, a disabled button made some customers think they’d done something wrong.

Seamless interaction to purchase

  • Show “ship to store” once color and size are selected
  • Display options as radio buttons
  • Keep the call to action always active

The delivery card adapts to how precise the date can be:

Design walkthrough

Last stop: confirming

The order details page keeps the experience seamless end to end. Right after ordering, the most relevant details are available at a glance, ordered by what customers told us matters: where to pick up, when the store is open, and how long the store will hold the package.
